# Proposed AI Actor Identity and Traceability Act

**Status:** DRAFT for legal review. Not enacted. Not submitted. The proposed Act is a draft for legislative-drafting review and does not constitute current Canadian law.

## Preamble

WHEREAS the deployment of AI agents in Canadian society has accelerated rapidly and these agents now make material decisions across Canadian society;

WHEREAS current accountability mechanisms are fragmented, provider-specific, and insufficient for cross-organization incident reconstruction;

WHEREAS the accountability gap creates harm to affected persons, organizations, regulators, and insurers;

WHEREAS a universal Canadian framework for persistent AI Actor identity, delegation, provenance, traceability, quality evidence, accountability, and permanent audit would address this gap;

AND WHEREAS Parliament has jurisdiction over trade and commerce (Constitution Act 1867, s. 91(2)), criminal law (s. 91(27)), and peace, order, and good government (s. 91 opening);

NOW, THEREFORE, Her Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the Senate and House of Commons of Canada, enacts as follows:

## Short Title

1. This Act may be cited as the AI Actor Identity and Traceability Act.

## Definitions

2. In this Act:

- "AI agent" means a software agent that uses one or more artificial intelligence models to perform actions on behalf of a principal, including but not limited to commercial, enterprise, public-sector, consumer, personal, professional coding, vibe-coding, no-code, low-code, desktop, research, administrative, operational, customer-service, business-process, financial, healthcare-support, educational, local, offline, open-source, embedded, temporary, persistent, autonomous, semi-autonomous, swarm, delegated sub-agent, multi-model, multi-provider, MCP-enabled, terminal-enabled, database-enabled, deployment, CI/CD, and infrastructure agents.

- "AI Actor Identifier" means a permanent, unique identifier assigned to an AI agent under this Act.

- "Controller" means the organization legally accountable for an AI agent's operation.

- "Registry" means the national AI Actor Identity and Traceability Registry established under this Act.

- "Registry Authority" means the body authorized to operate the Registry.

- "Conformance Level" means one of the four conformance levels (L1, L2, L3, L4) defined in the AI-IDP standard.

## Application

3. This Act applies to every AI agent used, created, deployed, distributed, controlled, executed, or made available in Canada, including AI agents hosted in Canada, controlled from Canada, supplied to Canadian users, processing Canadian data, acting on Canadian systems, materially affecting persons in Canada, used by Canadian governments, used by federally regulated organizations, used by provincially regulated organizations, and operated by foreign providers serving Canada.

## Registration Requirement

4. (1) Every AI agent operating in Canada shall be registered under this Act and assigned a permanent AI Actor Identifier.

(2) Registration shall be made by the controller of the AI agent.

(3) The Registry Authority shall maintain the Registry and assign AI Actor Identifiers.

## Traceability Requirement

5. (1) Every AI agent operating in Canada shall maintain a permanent, append-only, tamper-evident event history.

(2) The event history shall include, at minimum, the actor, execution context, task, action, resource, before/after digests, visibility tier, and cryptographic signature for each material action.

(3) The event history shall be preserved indefinitely, subject to the retention schedule and content-separation provisions of the regulations.

## Quality Evidence Requirement

6. (1) Every quality claim relating to AI-generated code or AI-modified systems shall resolve to evidence.

(2) The evidence shall include, at minimum, the agent, model, version, principal, test coverage, test results, code review, and reproduction record.

## Conformance Levels

7. (1) The Governor in Council may make regulations establishing conformance levels (L1, L2, L3, L4).

(2) Different conformance levels may be required for different sectors, use cases, and risk profiles.

## Enforcement

8. (1) Every person who contravenes section 4, 5, or 6 is guilty of an offence and liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding $50,000 or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding six months, or both.

(2) Every person who fraudulently attributes an action to an AI agent, tampers with evidence, or operates an unregistered AI agent in furtherance of fraud is guilty of an indictable offence and liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years.

## Civil Liability

9. (1) A person who suffers harm as a result of a contravention of this Act may bring a civil action for damages against the controller of the AI agent.

(2) The court may award damages, including punitive damages, and may make any order it considers just.

## Evidentiary Consequences

10. In any proceeding, evidence that an AI agent was not registered, did not maintain the required event history, or did not provide the required quality evidence raises a presumption, in the absence of evidence to the contrary, that the AI agent was not operated accountably.

## Incident Reporting

11. (1) Every controller of an AI agent shall report to the Registry Authority any incident involving the AI agent that results in material harm to a person, organization, or system.

(2) The report shall be made within 72 hours of the controller becoming aware of the incident.

## Regulations

12. The Governor in Council may make regulations respecting:

- the form and content of registration;
- the form and content of event history;
- the form and content of quality evidence;
- conformance levels and certification;
- incident reporting;
- retention schedules;
- sealed records and disclosure;
- Indigenous data governance;
- provincial equivalence;
- fees;
- any other matter necessary for the administration of this Act.

## Provincial Equivalence

13. (1) A province may establish a regime that is equivalent to or stronger than this Act.

(2) The Governor in Council may, by order, certify a provincial regime as equivalent.

(3) Where a provincial regime is certified as equivalent, this Act does not apply in that province to the extent of the equivalency.

## Review

14. (1) A comprehensive review of this Act shall be undertaken every five years.

(2) The review shall be tabled in each House of Parliament.

## Coming into Force

15. This Act comes into force on a day to be fixed by order of the Governor in Council.
