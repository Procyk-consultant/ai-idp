# Administrative Implementation Plan

Last Material Revision: 2026-08-01

## Governance Bodies

- National AI-IDP Registry Authority (operator of public and controlled tiers)
- Sectoral Registry Authorities (for regulated sectors: finance, healthcare, public sector, critical infrastructure)
- Provincial Registry Authorities (where provinces establish equivalent regimes)
- Certification Body Accreditation Authority (e.g., Standards Council of Canada)
- Auditor Accreditation Authority
- Incident Coordinator (national coordinator for cross-sectoral AI incidents)
- Civil-Society Oversight Board
- Parliamentary Oversight Committee

## Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|------|-----------------|
| Registry Authority | Operate registry; assign identifiers; maintain registry integrity; report to regulator |
| Certification Body | Audit; issue certifications; revoke certifications; report to regulator |
| Auditor | Perform audits; sign audit reports; report findings to controller and regulator |
| Incident Coordinator | Coordinate cross-sectoral incident response; report to regulator |
| Regulator | Enforce conformance; investigate incidents; impose penalties; report to Parliament |
| Civil-Society Oversight Board | Review implementation; report to Parliament |
| Parliamentary Oversight Committee | Review implementation; hold hearings; recommend amendments |

## Registration Process

1. Controller submits registration application.
2. Registry Authority validates application.
3. Registry Authority assigns permanent AI Actor Identifier.
4. Registry Authority records registration as signed event.
5. Registry Authority returns identifier to controller.

## Certification Process

1. Controller submits certification application.
2. Certification Body assigns auditor.
3. Auditor performs conformance audit.
4. Certification Body reviews audit report.
5. Certification Body issues certification (or denies).
6. Certification is recorded in public tier.

## Audit Process

1. Auditor opens audit with defined scope and procedure.
2. Auditor collects evidence.
3. Auditor verifies evidence.
4. Auditor documents findings.
5. Auditor signs audit report.
6. Audit report is recorded as signed event.

## Incident Process

1. Incident is reported.
2. Incident Coordinator opens investigation.
3. Coordinator collects evidence.
4. Coordinator reconstructs timeline.
5. Coordinator identifies root cause.
6. Coordinator documents findings.
7. Coordinator implements remediation.
8. Coordinator verifies remediation.
9. Coordinator closes incident.
10. Regulator is notified if high-severity.

## Service Standards

- Registration: 5 business days.
- Certification: 60 business days.
- Audit: 90 business days.
- Incident response: 24 hours (initial), 72 hours (regulator notification).
- Public records access: real-time.
- Controlled records access: 5 business days.
- Sealed records access: per judicial or regulator authority.

## Budget Categories

- Registry operations (personnel, infrastructure, security).
- Certification body accreditation.
- Auditor accreditation.
- Incident coordination.
- Regulator operations.
- Civil-society oversight.
- Research and development.
- Public consultation.
- Indigenous consultation and engagement.
- Small-developer and open-source support.
