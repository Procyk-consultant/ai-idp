# AI-IDP Research Protocol

Last Material Revision: 2026-08-01

## Research Questions

RQ1: What conceptual model distinguishes the persistent agent from the agent instance, the model, the provider, the principal, and the controller?

RQ2: What invariants must hold for a universal AI accountability framework?

RQ3: How can permanent identifiers remain resolvable after termination, revocation, provider closure, model retirement, repository transfer, and organizational restructuring?

RQ4: How can an append-only, tamper-evident, sequence-aware event history be implemented using established cryptographic primitives?

RQ5: How can the framework balance permanent traceability with privacy, human rights, and Indigenous data governance?

RQ6: How can the framework be enforced through Canadian law?

RQ7: How can the framework accommodate small developers, open-source projects, offline agents, and cross-organization delegation without weakening the invariants?

## Search Strategy

- Canadian primary sources (Parliament of Canada, Department of Justice, OPC, Treasury Board, ISED, CAISI, Public Safety, CSE, Library and Archives Canada, SCC, provincial legislatures and privacy regulators, CanLII)
- Technical primary sources (W3C, IETF, ISO, IEC, NIST, CNCF, OpenSSF, SPIFFE, in-toto, SLSA, Sigstore, OpenTelemetry)
- Academic sources (arXiv, IEEE, ACM, Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar, SSRN, legal journals, security conferences)
- Prior-art sources (patents, academic papers, standards, open-source repositories, commercial documentation)
- Secondary sources (for terminology discovery, source discovery, competing interpretations, context)

## Source Hierarchy

1. Primary sources preferred over secondary.
2. Official sources preferred over unofficial.
3. Recent sources preferred over older for fast-moving topics.
4. Foundational sources citation-chained to depth 1-2.
5. Contradictions searched and preserved.
6. Limitations documented.

## Screening Criteria

- Inclusion: primary source relevant to AI identity, provenance, traceability, audit, registry governance, Canadian AI governance, Canadian privacy, administrative law, security, societal impact, business operations.
- Exclusion: secondary source when primary is available; outdated source superseded by newer; source without persistent identifier; source in inaccessible language.

## Critical Appraisal

Each source is appraised for: authority, date, scope, methodology, funding, conflict disclosures, quality assessment, applicability, validation status.

## Synthesis Methods

- Domain evidence tables
- Critical-appraisal records
- Domain synthesis reports
- Cross-domain synthesis
- Contradiction analysis

## Contradiction Research

Search for evidence that: universal agent registration is unconstitutional; the federal government lacks jurisdiction; provincial jurisdiction prevents a national model; permanent traceability violates privacy law; permanent IDs enable surveillance; employee tracking becomes abusive; public registries create security targets; private registries create unaccountable power; logs can be bypassed; agents can falsify attribution; providers can conceal model changes; signatures do not prove model execution; privileged administrators can erase evidence; GitHub is not sufficiently durable; indefinite preservation is technically unsustainable; long-term cryptography becomes obsolete; storage costs are excessive; compliance harms small developers; compliance harms open source; compliance prevents innovation; foreign providers cannot be effectively regulated; offline agents cannot be governed; the standard duplicates existing systems; the integrated contribution is not novel; the system creates false confidence; the enforcement model is impractical; users can be falsely blamed for agent actions; agents can be falsely blamed for user actions; provider liability is misallocated; permanent records conflict with Indigenous data sovereignty; the system harms marginalized communities; the system concentrates power.

## Stopping Criteria

- All planned channels searched.
- All major terminology families searched.
- Foundational sources citation-chained.
- Contradictory evidence searched.
- Major claims have support or explicit gaps.
- Two successive search iterations reveal no new major legal, technical, governance, security, social, or operational category.
- Stopping rationale recorded.

## Stopping Rationale

The research stopped after two successive iterations revealed no new major category. The research covered: digital identity, provenance, audit, Canadian AI governance, Canadian privacy, administrative law, registry governance, security, societal impact, business operations, Indigenous data governance, international AI governance. Citations were chained to depth 1-2 for primary Canadian sources. Contradictions were searched and preserved (see CONTRADICTION_REGISTER.md).
