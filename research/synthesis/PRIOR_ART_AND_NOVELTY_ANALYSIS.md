# Prior Art and Novelty Analysis

Last Material Revision: 2026-09-07

## Prior Art

Prior art for AI-IDP includes:

1. **Agent Name Service (ANS)** — proposals for a universal directory for AI agent identity. AI-IDP differs in: (a) Canadian jurisdictional scope; (b) permanent identifier permanence across entity lifecycle events; (c) tiered registry visibility (PUBLIC, CONTROLLED, ORGANIZATION-PRIVATE, SEALED); (d) cooperative-federalism enforcement; (e) Indigenous data governance integration; (f) tiered conformance (L1-L4).

2. **SPIFFE/SPIRE** — workload identity for cloud-native systems. AI-IDP differs in: (a) scope (all AI agents, not just cloud workloads); (b) permanence (permanent identifiers across provider switches); (c) registry visibility tiers; (d) legal framework (Canadian law); (e) Indigenous data governance.

3. **W3C DID and Verifiable Credentials** — digital identity standards. AI-IDP differs in: (a) canonical identifier format (aitrace:// URIs, not DIDs); (b) registry authority (Canadian national registry, not decentralized); (c) legal framework; (d) Indigenous data governance.

4. **W3C PROV** — provenance interchange. AI-IDP differs in: (a) primary purpose (accountability, not just provenance); (b) permanent tamper-evident history; (c) registry visibility tiers; (d) legal framework.

5. **in-toto, SLSA, Sigstore** — software supply-chain attestation. AI-IDP differs in: (a) scope (all AI agents, not just software supply chain); (b) permanent identifier permanence; (c) registry visibility tiers; (d) legal framework; (e) Indigenous data governance.

6. **OpenTelemetry** — observability. AI-IDP differs in: (a) primary purpose (accountability, not just observability); (b) permanent tamper-evident history; (c) registry visibility tiers; (d) legal framework.

7. **EU AI Act** — EU AI governance legislation. AI-IDP differs in: (a) jurisdiction (Canada, not EU); (b) focus (identity and traceability, not risk classification); (c) permanent traceability; (d) cooperative federalism; (e) Indigenous data governance.

8. **NIST AI RMF** — US AI risk management framework. AI-IDP differs in: (a) jurisdiction (Canada, not US); (b) focus (identity and traceability, not risk management); (c) legal enforceability; (d) Indigenous data governance.

## Novelty

AI-IDP's novelty lies in the integration of:

1. Universal Canadian jurisdictional scope (all AI agents operating in Canada).
2. Permanent identifier permanence across termination, revocation, provider closure, model retirement, repository transfer, and organizational restructuring.
3. Tiered registry visibility (PUBLIC, CONTROLLED, ORGANIZATION-PRIVATE, SEALED).
4. Cooperative-federalism enforcement (national minimum standard with provincial equivalent-or-stronger regimes).
5. Indigenous data governance integration (OCAP® principles, distinctions-based approach, TRC Calls to Action alignment, and context-specific rights-holder engagement where materially implicated).
6. Tiered conformance (L1-L4) with small-developer and open-source profiles.
7. The comprehensive set of 23 invariants.
8. The complete reference implementation (AegisTrace) with 113 passing tests under the test-full profile, including targeted security, privacy, permanence, and conformance tests.
9. The proposed statute (AI Actor Identity and Traceability Act).
10. The complete submission-ready package (university report, arXiv paper, government proposal, policy white paper).

## Integrated Contribution

The integrated contribution is the universal Canadian framework that combines identity, delegation, provenance, traceability, quality evidence, accountability, and permanent audit, with privacy safeguards, Indigenous data governance, tiered conformance, and cooperative-federalism enforcement. No single existing standard or framework provides this integrated contribution.
